Understand The Basics

What is a carbon footprint?

A carbon footprint is an estimate of the greenhouse gas emissions associated with the activities we perform in everyday life. Transportation, electricity, food, flights and waste can all contribute to it.

🚗

Transportation

Cars, bikes and public transportation can create different levels of emissions depending on distance and travel method.

⚡

Energy

Electricity consumption contributes to your footprint depending on how much energy you use.

✈️

Travel

Air travel can contribute significantly to annual greenhouse gas emissions.

🌱

Lifestyle

Food choices and waste management also contribute to your overall environmental impact.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How does EcoTrack calculate my Eco Score? +
EcoTrack uses the estimated annual emissions from your transportation, electricity, flights, diet and waste inputs. Lower estimated emissions produce a higher educational Eco Score, capped between 10 and 100.
What is the Eco Score? +
Eco Score is an educational indicator that summarizes your estimated environmental impact and helps you understand opportunities for improvement.
What is the difference between carbon footprint and carbon emissions? +
Carbon emissions are greenhouse gases released by a source or activity. A carbon footprint is an estimate of the emissions associated with a person, activity, product or organisation.
Can I reduce my carbon footprint? +
Yes. Changes such as using lower-emission transport, reducing unnecessary energy consumption and managing waste responsibly can help reduce your impact.
Is my result exact? +
No. The calculator provides an estimate based on the information entered. Actual emissions can vary depending on location, technology and individual circumstances.

INDIA

India's Climate & Carbon Position

India is a major emitter in absolute terms, while its per-capita emissions remain below those of many developed economies. India's climate strategy combines development, mitigation and adaptation.

India: key climate-policy indicators

Government of India policy commitments
INDIA
IndicatorIndia
Net-zero target2070
Non-fossil installed electricity capacity target500 GW by 2030
Emissions-intensity target45% reduction from 2005 level by 2030
Additional carbon sink commitment2.5–3 billion tonnes CO₂e

Policy information: India's updated Nationally Determined Contribution and Government of India climate-policy sources. Review date: 9 September 2026.

🇮🇳 Development challenge

India needs to expand energy access and economic opportunity while reducing the emissions intensity of growth.

☀️ Energy transition

Solar, wind, hydro and other non-fossil sources are central to India's electricity-transition strategy.

🌳 Adaptation & carbon sinks

Forests, ecosystems, agriculture, water and climate-resilient infrastructure are important parts of India's response.

GOVERNMENT INITIATIVES

What initiatives has India taken?

India's climate response is distributed across national missions and multiple ministries and departments.

☀️

National Solar Mission

Promotes large-scale solar deployment and supports India's renewable-energy transition.

Parent Ministry: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)

Mission approved: 30 June 2008 • launched: 11 January 2010
⚡

National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency

Promotes energy efficiency and lower energy consumption across the economy.

Parent Agency: Bureau of Energy Efficiency, Ministry of Power

🏙️

National Mission on Sustainable Habitat

Addresses sustainable urban development, buildings, mobility, waste and water-related urban issues.

Parent Ministry: Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs

💧

National Water Mission

Promotes water conservation, efficiency and sustainable water-resource management.

Parent Ministry: Ministry of Jal Shakti

🏔️

National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem

Supports research, monitoring, ecosystem understanding and climate resilience in the Himalayan region.

Parent Ministry: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change

🌳

National Mission for a Green India

Focuses on forest and tree cover, restoration of degraded landscapes and carbon sequestration.

Parent Ministry: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change

Officially launched: February 2014
🌾

National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture

Supports climate-resilient agriculture through soil, water, agroforestry, rainfed-area and sustainable-farming measures.

Parent Department: Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare

Launched: 23 September 2010
🔬

National Mission on Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change

Builds climate knowledge, vulnerability assessment, modelling, capacity and scientific support for decision-making.

Parent Ministry: Ministry of Science and Technology • Implementing agency: Department of Science and Technology

🏥

National Programme on Climate Change & Human Health

Addresses climate-sensitive health risks such as heat, air pollution, disease surveillance and health-system resilience.

Parent Ministry: Ministry of Health and Family Welfare

🌐

National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC)

Provides the overarching national framework connecting India's climate missions and initiatives.

Nodal / coordinating Ministry: Department of Science and Technology

Launched: 2008

PARENT DEPARTMENTS

Who is responsible for what?

Climate action in India is shared across ministries, departments and implementing agencies.

Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change

Nodal/coordinating point for the National Action Plan on Climate Change and coordination of its missions and wider climate-policy work.

Ministry of New and Renewable Energy

Leads major renewable-energy programmes, including the National Solar Mission.

Ministry of Power / Bureau of Energy Efficiency

Responsible for power-sector policy and major energy-efficiency programmes.

Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare

Leads the sustainable-agriculture mission and climate-resilient agriculture measures.

Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs

Leads sustainable-habitat and urban-development measures linked with climate resilience.

Ministry of Jal Shakti

Leads national water-conservation and sustainable water-management efforts.

Ministry of Science and Technology / Department of Science and Technology

Anchors strategic climate knowledge, research, modelling and capacity-building programmes.

Ministry of Health and Family Welfare

Leads climate-and-health programmes addressing health risks from a changing climate.

ANALYSIS

What does the data tell us?

01 — Global picture

Global emissions increased substantially over the long term. Future reductions require changes across energy, transport, industry, buildings, agriculture and land use.

02 — India's position

India's climate challenge is closely connected with development, energy access and rising demand, so reducing emissions intensity is important.

03 — Government response

India uses a multi-ministry approach. Different missions address energy, forests, agriculture, water, cities, health and climate knowledge.

04 — Why targets matter

Targets such as 500 GW of non-fossil capacity and net zero by 2070 provide long-term direction for policy and investment.

05 — Why dates matter

The newest available emissions inventory or assessment year may lag behind the current calendar year. The page therefore labels the actual data period.

06 — Individual action

Transport, electricity, food, travel and waste choices can influence an individual's estimated footprint, which is why EcoTrack connects national context to personal action.
